Adaptive TDS: Time Delay Stability that sets its own parameters

TDS needs a window, a lag range and a tolerance, and for a new system nobody knows the right values. aTDS takes only the signals and measures all three from them.

The problem

Time Delay Stability (Bashan et al. 2012) links two signals when the delay τ₀ that best aligns them stays nearly constant from window to window: a window counts as stable when 4 of 5 neighbouring delays agree within a tolerance, and the TDS score is the share of stable windows.

The published setting, window 60, lag ±30, tolerance ±1, was chosen for 1‑Hz sleep recordings and is counted in samples. For a slower system, or the same system recorded faster, the same numbers mean something else, and TDS can miss a coupling that is plainly there.

The idea: three rules

ParameterRuleWhy
WindowL = 30 · BB (the Bartlett factor, from the autocorrelation) says how many samples make one independent sample, so every window holds about 30 independent samples of the slowest signal. Never shorter than 61 samples.
Lag range±(L − 1) / 2The published ratio (±30 in 60), so the search grows with the window; capped at half a rhythm's period if a signal is rhythmic.
Tolerancechance ≤ 5 %One signal is rotated in time to destroy the coupling; the tolerance is the widest one at which these coupling-free copies look stable in at most 5 % of windows.

The 4-of-5 stability rule is unchanged. The only required input is the signals; the longest plausible delay can be given as an option, and then the window is made long enough to search that far.

What the tests show

All tests use synthetic signals with a known coupling and delay, so a method's answer can be checked. aTDS sets its parameters on separate training data and is scored on data it has not seen.

Verdict

  • Much better than the published setting whenever the system is slower than that setting assumes; equal where it fits.
  • As good as an expert who knows the time scale, without being told it; better in one case, undecided in two (Figure 3).
  • Not optimal. A setting tuned on labelled data does better; aTDS's value is that it needs no tuning.
  • False alarms stay within budget: on new uncoupled pairs the chance score was 3.3–4.4 %, and a per-link test on networks reported no false link in 20 uncoupled networks.
  • Also better on long-memory signals (H = 0.9), rhythms with a delay below half a period, and signals whose dynamics change halfway; equal on long-memory signals with H = 0.6 and 0.75; detects bursting neurons but reports their delay 3 steps too long.

Limits and open questions

  • Long delays in fast signals are missed silently unless the optional longest delay is given (delay 50: AUC 0.56 without it, 1.00 with it). Rhythms with a delay beyond half a period give a wrong delay.
  • Coarser timing: longer windows mean one answer per window, so short coupling episodes can be missed; a maximum window can be set.
  • The delay is known only within the tolerance, which grows for slow signals (up to ±56 here).
  • Untested so far: whether aTDS changes or sharpens sleep-stage contrasts in real sleep networks; the cross-subject, stage-matched surrogates of the original method; coupling that switches on and off with the sleep stage.
