THOUGHT:
The paper addresses a real and practically relevant gap: Time Delay Stability (TDS) has three parameters (window L, lag range tau_max, tolerance tol) expressed in samples and tuned for 1-Hz sleep series, and there is no rule for transferring them to other time scales or sampling rates. The proposed aTDS replaces them by (i) a window holding ~30 Bartlett-effective independent samples of the slowest signal, (ii) a lag range of (L-1)/2 (the alias-free limit of circular cross-correlation), capped at half a detected rhythm period, and (iii) a tolerance calibrated on circularly rotated, coupling-free copies so that the mean chance score plus 2 SE stays below a 5 % window-level budget.

My overall intuition: this is a careful, unusually honest methods paper whose individual ingredients are not new (Bartlett/effective sample size, surrogate calibration, Nyquist-type lag limit), and whose central empirical message is partly self-undermining in a scientifically healthy way: the authors show that a crude one-factor, data-driven rescaling of fixed TDS captures most of the detection gain (and is slightly higher in AUC at r = 2 and 4). The distinctive contributions of aTDS beyond rescaling are therefore (a) the calibrated tolerance, which controls the control-pair level (very visible in the ECG data: rescaled controls reach a 95th percentile of 39 % vs 1.3 % for aTDS), (b) the rhythm cap, and (c) the demonstration that a fixed lag range fails silently with high scores and wrong (even sign-flipped) delays. Point (c) is a useful, clearly demonstrated cautionary result for the TDS community, even though it holds "by construction"; the value is in showing that the score gives no warning.

Points I want to scrutinise:
1. Tolerance growth and the meaning of "stability". The calibrated tolerance scales with the time scale (±13 for tau_max ±129; ±56 at r = 16). A tolerance of several percent of the lag range makes "stable delay" a much weaker statement than in the original method, and the calibrated budget (≈4 % false stability) is roughly 4-5x the false stability of fixed TDS in the delay-pattern tests (≈0.8-0.9 %). The authors acknowledge that aTDS scores are not comparable to legacy TDS values, but the consequence is that AUC comparisons partly compare different operating points. A comparison at matched false-stability levels (e.g., fixed/rescaled TDS with its tolerance calibrated to the same budget) would isolate the window/lag effect from the operating-point effect.
2. The window constant 30 is acknowledged to be a convention; the oracle shows longer windows are better for permanent coupling and the sleep cohort shows the uncapped window (48 min) is useless for stage resolution. The rule therefore needs a user cap in exactly the scenarios (stage-resolved, seasonal, rhythmic) where TDS is most used. This narrows the practical claim of "data-driven" parameter selection.
3. Bartlett truncation at the first zero crossing is a heuristic; it fails on seasonal data (L0123002). The Cauchy-Schwarz bound argument is for the untruncated sums and is correctly qualified.
4. Delay accuracy: within-window overlap shrinks at long delays (scores drop to 27-62 % at d = 160), and with circular correlation, delays close to L/2 involve heavy wrap-around; it would be good to quantify bias/variance of tau_0 near the edge of the range. In ECG, aTDS agrees less often than fixed TDS with the beat-by-beat pulse arrival time; the chosen reference (full-record cross-correlation peak) is itself a cross-correlation estimator, which may favour long-window methods.
5. Evaluation breadth: synthetic tests are mostly linear AR(1)-Gaussian with linear coupling (other classes relegated to supplement); real-data domains give 3 of 5 recoveries and the cohort shows no gain. The ECG comparison against fixed TDS at native 125 Hz is something of a straw man (acknowledged). The rescaled baseline is evaluated only in the slow-down test and ECG.
6. Rotation null: fine for preserving autocorrelation, but for strongly periodic pairs the rotated copies can remain phase-aligned, and calibration fails (acknowledged). The window-level budget is not a link-level test; link significance is left to a separate procedure, so the "false-stability budget" should not be confused with error control on networks.
7. Presentation: dense, but clear and well-structured; extensive hedging is commendable. Some numbers live only in the supplement (oracle, manual rescaling, per-pair ECG), and the notation of units in Table 4 mixes seconds and samples.

Desired outcome: the main claims (fixed parameters fail silently out of regime; aTDS restores detection and correct delays; tolerance holds the budget on new data) are supported by the evidence presented and the limitations are stated candidly. Originality and significance are moderate: the paper is mainly an engineering/standardisation contribution for a niche but active community (network physiology). I lean towards a weak accept (6), with confidence 4.
