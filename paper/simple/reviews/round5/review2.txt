THOUGHT:
The paper proposes adaptive TDS (aTDS): three data-driven rules for the window length (30 Bartlett-effective samples of the slowest signal), the lag range ((L-1)/2, capped at half a rhythm period) and the stability tolerance (largest t keeping the C+2SE chance score of circularly rotated copies at or below 5 %). It is evaluated on AR(1) simulations (slow-down test, time-varying delays), a sleep-EEG benchmark pair, a 35-subject sleep cohort, 53 BIDMC ICU ECG/PPG recordings and five records from other fields.

My intuition: this is a careful, unusually candid engineering paper. The problem is real (sample-counted parameters carried over unchanged across sampling rates), and the silent-failure demonstration of a fixed lag range (high scores, wrong delay, sometimes wrong sign) is the most useful and convincing finding. The candour is commendable, but it also shows how thin the empirical case for the full method is relative to simple alternatives:
- The paper's own "rescaled fixed TDS" baseline, a one-parameter rescaling by an autocorrelation time, matches aTDS in AUC in the slow-down test (higher at r = 2 and 4, lower at r = 8, overlapping intervals everywhere) and on the ECG data (0.962 vs 0.963). The extra contribution of the per-parameter rules therefore rests mostly on the ECG control level and one r = 8 delay-recovery result.
- On 1-Hz sleep data, the design domain, aTDS gives no gain and loses power for wake.
- An oracle beats aTDS at every r >= 2. At r = 16 the upper-bound tolerance rule lowers AUC compared with simpler variants.
- In the ECG data, aTDS agrees with the beat-by-beat pulse arrival time less often than fixed TDS. In the other-field records, 2 of 5 delays are not recovered without manual intervention.
The paper itself says every ingredient (Bartlett effective sample size, circular rotation surrogates, half-window lag range) is standard. Novelty therefore lies in the combination and the evaluation, which makes this an incremental methodological contribution.

Technical concerns I want to raise:
1. The window rule rests on a first-zero-truncated Bartlett sum. The Cauchy-Schwarz justification for a common window does not hold for this estimator, and it fails for seasonal and long-memory signals; the constant 30 is admittedly a convention.
2. The tolerance calibration uses C + 2 SE pooled over rotations of the same (sometimes coupled) pairs. The null is rotation, which preserves autocorrelation but not nonstationarity. Its 5 % budget is a mean window-level quantity and does not control link-level error. Also, the choice of 2 SE was motivated post hoc by an observed 6.7 % overshoot.
3. ECG comparison: aTDS parameters are medians of per-pair values computed on the same recordings that are scored, and its tolerance is calibrated on rotations of the true pairs. No train/test split is used here, unlike in the simulations. Also, the rescaled baseline receives tol = ±8 samples, while aTDS gets tol = 0 from calibration, so the "controls reach high scores" result partly reflects the absence of calibration, which could be bolted onto the rescaled baseline. A rescaled-plus-calibrated-tolerance ablation is missing.
4. The non-causal anchor (look-ahead) is the default and inflates scores (58.1 % vs 43.5 % on the benchmark). This is fine for offline use, but it should be emphasised when comparing to the literature.
5. Simulations are dominated by linear AR(1) with additive delay. Nonlinear and non-Gaussian coupling is only in the supplement.
6. The rescaled baseline is evaluated only in two settings, so the general claim that "the additional rules matter for rhythmic signals and the control level" rests essentially on one dataset.

Desired outcome: the authors should (a) add ablations that combine rescaling with each additional rule, (b) use held-out parameter selection on the ECG data, (c) give a link-level error-control analysis, and (d) either reduce claims or show a setting where the full aTDS clearly beats the simple rescaling. The clarity is good overall but dense, with many important details deferred to nine supplementary sections. Overall, I judge it a borderline, honest but incremental paper with major remaining methodological gaps. I lean toward reject at a selective venue.
