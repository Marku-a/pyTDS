THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three data-driven rules for choosing the window length, lag range and stability tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS): (i) window = 30 x max_i B_i (Bartlett factor truncated at the first zero crossing), clipped to [61, 0.1T]; (ii) lag range = (L-1)/2, capped at half a rhythm period; (iii) tolerance = largest t such that the mean chance score of circularly rotated copies + 2 SE stays below 5 %. It is evaluated on synthetic AR(1)/fGn/AR(2)/Rulkov signals, a benchmark EEG pair, ECG-PPG at 125 Hz (BIDMC), a 35-subject sleep cohort, and hydrological/energy/fuel-price records.

My overall intuition: this is a careful, unusually candid engineering paper. The reporting is honest (Table 7 lists losses, the sleep cohort is reported as "no gain", the oracle is shown to dominate, the REM change is not claimed as improvement). The problem being addressed is real: TDS parameters are in samples and are routinely transplanted across sampling rates without justification, and the demonstration that a fixed +-30 lag range produces high TDS scores with wrong (even sign-flipped) delays is a useful cautionary result for the network-physiology community.

However, the methodological novelty is modest and the headline comparisons partly lean on a strawman. Each rule is an application of standard ideas (Bartlett/Bayley-Hammersley effective sample size, the circular-correlation Nyquist-type limit on lags, a rotation surrogate null). The authors themselves concede in the Limitations that simply multiplying the fixed parameters by a factor estimated from the correlation time "performed similarly to aTDS in the slow-down test", yet this simple baseline is not shown in any table or figure. Most of the "better" entries in Table 7 compare against fixed TDS in regimes for which fixed TDS was never intended (r >= 2, 125 Hz ECG with a 0.48 s window). Against the fair baseline (rescaled TDS), aTDS is equivalent at r = 1, 2, undecided at r = 4, 16 and better only at r = 8. The real question for a practitioner is "does aTDS beat a simple, obvious rescaling by an estimated time scale?" and the paper does not answer it with data.

The "explicit false-alarm budget" claim in the abstract and conclusions is overstated. The 5 % budget is the mean fraction of stable windows in coupling-free rotated copies, explicitly not the probability of a spurious link (as the authors admit in Sec. 2.6). The calibrated tolerance is also large: about one tenth of the lag range (e.g., +-13 for a delay of 20; +-136 s in the uncapped sleep setting), so "stability" is substantially diluted relative to the original +-1 sample definition, and false stability in controls rises from <1 % to 4-4.3 % (6.1 % next to coupled blocks). Effectively aTDS trades the original strict stability criterion for higher sensitivity, and the score becomes a different statistic, not comparable to the literature, which the authors acknowledge. Also, with M = 20 rotations and <= 50 pairs, C + 2SE is itself noisy, and its "new-data" calibration claim (3.3-4.4 %) is tested only on homogeneous synthetic data.

Further technical concerns: (a) The lag-range justification ("largest range the circular correlation permits without aliasing") is a consequence of choosing circular correlation in short windows; at lags near L/2 the genuinely overlapping part is only half the window and the wrap-around segment is not physically meaningful. A linear (zero-padded, overlap-normalised) correlation would allow a principled comparison; the drop of aTDS scores at long delays (27-62 % at d = 160) is a symptom. (b) The window rule uses first-zero truncation, which is ad hoc and fails for seasonal and long-memory signals (L0123002; B grows with T), so the "property of the record rather than the system" caveat is important. (c) The design replaces three parameters with many design constants (30 independent samples, 5 % budget, +2SE, M = 20, 50-pair cap, 0.3 rhythm threshold, Lmin = 61, 10 % cap, a = max(L, T/10), 80 % stage occupancy); only the constant 30 receives a (single-configuration) sensitivity analysis. (d) The simulations are mostly linear Gaussian unidirectional couplings with matched noise memory; common-driver confounds, bidirectional coupling, and nonstationary/heteroscedastic physiology are not tested; a wide tolerance plus long windows plausibly increases spurious links from shared slow trends. (e) The non-causal stability anchor changes the benchmark score from 43.53 % to 58.1 %; the abstract cites the causal reproduction while the rest of the paper uses the non-causal variant, which needs explanation since it is a large effect on a supposedly identical algorithm.

Real-data evidence is mixed. The ECG-PPG result is the strongest, but the coupling is trivially strong (same heartbeat), the fixed baseline is a 0.48 s window that nobody would use at 125 Hz, parameters were set per pair (unlike the "common scale" principle stated in Sec. 2.3), calibration failed in 12/53 pairs, and delay agreement with the beat-by-beat pulse arrival time is worse than fixed TDS (24/53 vs 32/41). The sleep cohort shows no gain and one significant loss (wake contrast). For the non-physiological records, aTDS recovers delays that a full-record cross-correlation already gives; no comparison to that trivial estimator or to standard time-varying delay estimators (e.g., GCC-PHAT in sliding windows, wavelet coherence phase) is offered, so the added value outside physiology is not demonstrated.

Clarity: dense, number-heavy writing; the main text references many figures whose message must be re-read in prose; sign conventions (negative tau0 means x leads, reported delay = -tau0) add friction. The validation design itself is well described and code is promised.

Overall: a useful, honest, practically oriented contribution to TDS methodology with sound simulation design, but limited novelty, key fair baseline missing from the results, an overstated false-alarm framing, and real-data gains concentrated in a setting where the fixed baseline is obviously mis-specified. I lean to reject in its current form (5), with a clear path to acceptance if the rescaling baseline, sensitivity analyses and linear-correlation/confound tests are added and the claims are tempered.
