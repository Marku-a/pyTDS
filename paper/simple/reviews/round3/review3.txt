THOUGHT:
The paper proposes adaptive TDS (aTDS): three data-driven rules for the window (30 x the largest first-zero-truncated Bartlett factor, clipped to [61, 0.1T]), the lag range (half the window, capped at half a detected rhythm period) and the stability tolerance (largest t for which the mean + 2 SE chance score of circularly rotated copies stays below 5 %). The 4-of-5 rule is unchanged. It is evaluated on synthetic AR(1)/fGn/AR(2)/Rulkov signals, on the EEG benchmark pair, on 53 BIDMC ECG/PPG recordings at 125 Hz, on a 35-subject sleep cohort, and on hydrological/energy/fuel records.

My intuitions:
- The problem is real and well motivated: TDS parameters are defined in samples, and the lag-range experiment (Fig. 4) gives a clean demonstration that a fixed +/-30 search yields high scores with wrong (sometimes sign-flipped) delays. This is a useful warning for practitioners.
- The method itself is a combination of standard ingredients (Bartlett/Bayley-Hammersley effective sample size, circular-shift surrogates, a half-period cap). The novelty is in the packaging for TDS rather than in any new statistical idea. The constant 30, the 0.3 rhythm threshold, Lmin = 61, the 10 % cap, the rotation exclusion zone and the 5 % budget are all heuristics; the sensitivity analysis (Table 2) is helpful but does not cover the 50-pair cap, the exclusion zone or the rhythm detector beyond one AR(2) case.
- The authors' own most important finding cuts against the significance: a crude one-factor rescaling from the 1/e correlation time (rescaled fixed TDS) captures essentially all of the detection gain (Table 3; AUC equal or higher at r = 2, 4 and on ECG). The incremental value of the two extra rules is demonstrated mainly in one dataset (ECG control levels, 39 % vs 1.3 % 95th percentile) and in delay recovery at r = 8. This is fairly narrow evidence for the claim that the per-parameter design is needed.
- In the design domain (1-Hz sleep networks) aTDS gives no gain, the uncapped window (48 min) cannot resolve sleep stages at all, and the significant-link wake contrast gets significantly worse. So the default procedure fails in the main application field of TDS unless the user imposes a cap, which reintroduces a manual parameter.
- The tolerance budget is a window-level mean chance score, not a link-level error rate; the authors say so, but the abstract's phrasing ("false-stability budget") may be over-read. The wide calibrated tolerances (up to ~10 % of the lag range) change the meaning of the score and make it non-comparable with prior TDS literature, which partly undermines the reproducibility motivation.
- The rotation null has issues: for periodic signals (ECG) rotated copies realign by chance (12/53 per-pair calibration failures), for nonstationary staged data rotation ignores stage structure, and the same rotation null is used both to choose the tolerance and to compute p-values on the same record. The authors use a cross-subject null for sleep, which is good, but the double use should be discussed.
- Experimental rigour is otherwise high: train/test separation for parameter choice, bootstrap CIs, TOST equivalence, an oracle upper bound, a summary table that includes the negative results, fixed seeds and released code. The honesty about limitations is notable.
- Presentation is accurate but very dense; the paper reads as a long list of numbers with nested caveats. The abstract is overloaded. Some definitions are underspecified (what is the "common value" in the 4-of-5 rule; how the rhythm peak "does not lie at the boundary"). The fact that the pyTDS default (non-causal anchor) does not reproduce the original MATLAB benchmark (58.1 % vs 43.6 %) is surprising and should be justified.
- Several comparisons against fixed TDS are partly straw-man by construction (fixed sample-based parameters at 125 Hz or r = 16); the authors acknowledge this.

Overall: claims are carefully stated and mostly supported; the originality and significance are moderate and limited to the TDS community; the main gain is reproducible by a simpler baseline. I place it at a 6 (borderline accept): a solid, honest, reproducible methods paper of modest novelty.
