THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, which sets three rules for the free parameters of Time Delay Stability (TDS): a window of 30 Bartlett-effective samples of the slowest signal, a lag range of (L-1)/2 capped at half a detected rhythm period, and a stability tolerance calibrated on circularly rotated surrogates so that the mean chance score plus 2 SE stays at or below 5 %. The problem is real. TDS parameters are fixed in samples, and the silent wrong-delay failure (high scores, wrong delay, sometimes the wrong sign) is a useful warning for the network-physiology community. The paper is unusually honest. It reports the cases where aTDS is worse or no better (the sleep cohort, the wake contrast, agreement with pulse arrival time, rhythms with delays beyond half a period, weekly fuel prices, the seasonal catchment). It also includes a fair data-driven rescaling baseline and an oracle.

My central concern is how much each part contributes. The paper itself shows that "rescaled fixed TDS", a one-factor rescaling from the 1/e correlation time, matches aTDS in AUC in the slow-down test and on the ECG data, and is slightly higher at r = 2 and 4. Most of the headline gain over fixed TDS (AUC 0.92 vs 0.59; 50/53 vs 14/53 patients) therefore comes from a baseline that is mismatched by construction. The authors acknowledge this. The new contributions are then the rhythm cap and the calibrated tolerance.
- The tolerance rule's own evidence is modest. The AUC gain is not significant, it is marginally worse at r = 1, and it helps delay recovery mainly at r = 8.
- The ECG advantage over rescaling shows up only as "patients above the controls' 95th percentile". AUC is identical. This is a threshold-dependent criterion: rescaled TDS lets uncoupled controls reach high scores because its tolerance and lag range are uncalibrated, which is just as easily fixed by calibrating the rescaled method's tolerance. An ablation is missing: rescaled TDS plus rotation-calibrated tolerance, and/or plus the rhythm cap.

Methodological points:
(1) The 5 % budget is on the mean window-level chance score, not a link-level false-positive rate. Link significance needs separate rotation tests, which are run only in some analyses. A calibrated tolerance of ±13 for a delay of 20 changes what "stable" means. Scores are then not comparable with the literature, and the paper concedes this.
(2) The window constant 30 is a convention. The oracle and Table 8 show that more samples per window (or longer windows with tolerance 0) detect clearly better. The trade-off with temporal resolution is shown only for one on/off design, so the rule does not optimise anything.
(3) Bartlett truncation at the first zero crossing fails for long-memory and seasonal signals; B grows with T. One max-B window per network penalises fast pairs (mixed-speed case, ±56 vs ±3 tolerance).
(4) The circular-rotation null keeps autocorrelation but not non-stationarity. In the ECG data the calibration failed for 12/53 pairs because the signals are quasi-periodic.
(5) The simulations are mostly linear AR(1) with additive delayed copies, which closely matches the correlation-based estimator. Nonlinear and non-monotonic coupling, noise in both channels and common drivers are tested only lightly (one network configuration).
(6) The non-causal stability anchor is the default but gives 58.1 % instead of the MATLAB 43.6 %. This raises a reproducibility issue in itself, since the original method appears to use the causal anchor.
(7) There is no comparison with standard full-record or windowed delay estimators (GCC/PHAT, mutual-information lag), or with alternative TDS implementations such as TDSpy.
(8) The many comparisons in Table 7 are non-independent and have no multiplicity control. The "better/equal/worse" coding mixes criteria.
(9) Presentation is very dense, with numbers in almost every sentence, and the evidence for each rule is spread across the main text and appendices. Its clarity is acceptable but tiring to read.

Overall this is a careful, reproducible engineering contribution with limited methodological novelty. Its main gain is largely explained by a simple rescaling, and there is no positive result in the design domain. I lean toward rejection at a general venue; a methods/software journal in network physiology would suit it better after the missing ablation.
