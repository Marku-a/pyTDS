THOUGHT:
The paper tackles a real and under-discussed practical problem: the three TDS parameters (L=60, tau_max=+-30, tol=+-1) are specified in samples and were tuned for 1-Hz sleep series. Applied unchanged at other sampling rates or time scales, they can fail without any warning. The lag-range experiment (Fig. 2) makes this concrete: high TDS scores (72-96 %) coexist with a delay pinned at the search boundary, sometimes with the wrong sign. I see this as the most useful and least disputable message of the paper.

The proposed rules are simple heuristics built from standard ingredients (Bartlett effective sample size, the aliasing limit of circular correlation, rotation surrogates). The author says openly that none of the ingredients is new. The main contribution is engineering and a validation protocol, not a new method. The most important honest finding is that a crude data-driven one-factor rescaling ("rescaled fixed TDS") recovers most of the detection gain. It is even slightly better in AUC at r=2 and r=4, and equal on the ECG data. This weakens the case for the full per-parameter machinery: the extra value of aTDS comes down to (i) the calibrated tolerance, which lowers control scores, and (ii) the rhythm cap, which keeps the reported delay unambiguous. Both are demonstrated mainly on the ECG/pulse data.

Technical concerns I want to raise:
1. Tolerance growth. The calibrated tolerance scales with the time scale (+-56 samples at r=16, +-13 for tau_c=8). At that point "stable delay" means agreement within a band about 100 samples wide, which dilutes the notion of a stable time delay. It also makes aTDS scores incomparable across datasets and with the literature (the author acknowledges this). Fig. 3b shows the coupled score barely depends on tol, so the tolerance mainly sets the false-stability level rather than detection. That is fine, but it should be framed that way.
2. False stability. aTDS deliberately runs at about 4 % false stability, against about 1 % for fixed TDS. AUC comparisons partly reflect a different operating point, and an ROC-at-matched-false-stability analysis would be fairer.
3. Synthetic validation. It is dominated by linear Gaussian AR(1) with a pure lag. Non-linear, non-stationary and multi-delay settings appear only in the supplement, so robustness claims rest on material I cannot fully assess.
4. ECG delay reference. The "full-record alignment" reference is itself a cross-correlation peak. That is partly circular for a cross-correlation-based method. Against the physiologically meaningful beat-by-beat pulse arrival time, fixed TDS agreed more often than aTDS. This is a notable negative for a method whose selling point is a correct delay, and it deserves more analysis rather than a supplement reference.
5. Window rule. It rests on a truncated Bartlett sum and the arbitrary constant 30. It fails for seasonal signals (L0123002), and the oracle beats it consistently. The window is admittedly a compromise, not an optimum.
6. Sleep cohort. On the physiology that motivated TDS, aTDS brings no gain and loses power for wake. The uncapped window is 48 min.
7. Other fields. The delay is recovered in 3 of 5 records. These are illustrative case studies, not a validation.

Strengths in rigor: training/scoring separation, held-out patient halves, paired bootstrap, equivalence tests (TOST), Wilson intervals, fixed seeds and open code. The limitations are reported unusually candidly. The writing is dense but precise. The notation table and algorithm box help. Some sentences pack many numbers, and frequent deferral to the supplement makes key evidence hard to check.

Overall, the major claims are supported, and the paper is careful and honest. Novelty and significance are moderate and confined largely to the TDS / network-physiology community, and the rescaling baseline shows most of the gain is simply "match the time scale". This is a borderline paper. I lean toward a weak accept as a useful, reproducible methods contribution, conditional on clarifying the matched-false-stability comparison and the delay-accuracy result against pulse arrival time.
