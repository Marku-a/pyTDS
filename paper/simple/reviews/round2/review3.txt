THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three data-driven rules for the window length, lag range and stability tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS): window = 30 x max_i Bartlett factor (first-zero truncated), lag range = (L-1)/2 with a half-period cap for rhythmic signals, and tolerance = largest t such that the mean + 2 SE chance score of circularly rotated copies stays below 5 %. The 4-of-5 stability rule is kept. Evaluation spans synthetic AR(1)/fGn/AR(2)/Rulkov signals, a 1-Hz EEG benchmark pair, a 35-subject sleep cohort, 53 BIDMC ECG/PPG recordings at 125 Hz, and hydrology/energy/fuel-price records.

My intuitions. (1) The paper is unusually candid: it reports where aTDS is equal or worse (1-Hz sleep regime, wake contrast, pulse-arrival-time reference, false stability 4 % vs <1 %), quantifies sensitivity to its design constants, separates calibration from scoring data, and gives bootstrap/Wilson/TOST statistics. That is to its credit. (2) But the core contribution is a heuristic rescaling of three parameters. Most of the headline wins are against fixed sample-based parameters applied far outside their design regime (e.g. 60 samples = 0.48 s at 125 Hz, less than one heartbeat), which is close to a strawman: no competent analyst would apply those values unchanged at 125 Hz. The relevant baseline is rescaling by an estimated time scale; the authors themselves state that this "performed similarly to aTDS in the slow-down test" and that it was "not compared in the other experiments". The manually rescaled reference (given the true r) is equivalent or undecided at most r, better for aTDS only at r = 8. So the incremental value of the specific rules over a one-line rescaling is not demonstrated on the real-data applications, which is exactly where it matters. (3) The rotation null is an elegant within-recording choice, but it breaks for quasi-periodic signals: in 12 of 53 ECG pairs calibration failed even at tolerance 0 because rotated heartbeat signals re-align by chance. The proposed fix (tolerance 0) is ad hoc. The "5 % false-stability budget" is a window-level mean, not a per-link error rate; this is stated, but it means the headline calibration has no direct inferential meaning and the link-level significance still relies on separate tests. (4) Internal inconsistency: Sec. 2.2 calls the non-causal anchor "the offline definition of Bashan et al.", yet the causal anchor is what reproduces the MATLAB benchmark (43.53 vs 43.61 %) while the non-causal anchor gives 58.1 %. Either the original implementation is causal or the description is wrong; the abstract's "reproduces the original TDS benchmark" uses a different anchor from every other analysis in the paper. (5) The Bartlett factor truncated at the first zero crossing lacks the guarantee the Cauchy–Schwarz argument gives for full sums, and the paper shows it fails on seasonal data (3-year window, 19 windows); the long-memory dependence on record length means the window is not a property of the system. (6) Delay accuracy claims ("99 % within 3 samples") are at strong coupling (c = 0.6); at weak coupling and large r the delay-found rate drops to 42 %. Tolerances up to ~10 % of the lag range (e.g. ±136 s in the uncapped sleep setting) make aTDS scores incomparable with the TDS literature, which the paper acknowledges but which weakens the network-physiology use case. (7) Sleep results: aTDS gives no gain, loses the wake contrast, and the uncapped window cannot resolve stages at all, requiring an analyst-chosen cap — i.e., in the field's main use case the method needs manual parameters anyway. (8) Table 8's vote count (14 of 21 "better") mixes non-independent comparisons and isolated-rule tests against single fixed values; it overstates the evidence. (9) Clarity: very dense, number-heavy prose; sign conventions (tau0 vs reported delay) are confusing; some figures are referenced with details only in captions. Sleep cohort data are not public.

Overall: well executed and honest, practically useful as a software default, but modest originality and the key baseline (estimated rescaling) is missing from most experiments; several real-data wins are against an unrealistic configuration. I lean towards rejection in its current form, with a clear path to acceptance (estimated-rescaling and per-pair joint-Bartlett baselines on all real data, resolve the anchor inconsistency, a robust null for rhythmic signals).
