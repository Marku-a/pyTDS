THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three heuristic rules for choosing the window, lag range and tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS) from the data. The window holds about 30 "independent samples" according to a truncated Bartlett-type factor. The lag range is half the window, capped at half a detected rhythm period. The tolerance is the widest value whose chance score on circularly rotated copies stays under a 5 % budget. The evaluation is unusually extensive and unusually candid. The paper reports where the method loses: to full-record cross-correlation and GCC-PHAT, to an oracle, to a simple rescaling by an estimated correlation time, on regime-limited coupling, on non-monotone coupling, on one seasonal catchment, and on the 35-subject sleep cohort, where it gives no gain. That honesty is a real strength, and the experimental hygiene is good: paired bootstrap, TOST equivalence, held-out validation of the BH variant, train/test separation for calibration, and an FDR network test.

My main concerns are significance and novelty against the strongest baselines.
(1) The window rule, the core idea, amounts to rescaling the published setting by an autocorrelation time. The paper itself shows that a one-line rescaling by an estimated r-hat matches first-zero aTDS on the slow-down benchmark and on several other tests. It also beats aTDS on the rhythm with d=30 and on false stability under regime switching.
(2) The headline advantage in the abstract comes from aTDS-BH. This estimator was picked by comparing estimators on the same slow-down benchmark. It was validated on held-out data drawn from the same generator family with new seeds. That is weak out-of-distribution evidence. aTDS-BH also exceeds the 5 % budget at r=8 and r=16 and fails under regime switching. Its gain is mostly "longer windows detect better", which the oracle and the sensitivity table (45–60 samples) already show. So the BH advantage may simply reflect a longer effective window, not a principled estimator.
(3) Statistical naming and rationale are loose:
- The "Bartlett factor" 1+2Σr² truncated at the first zero is not the effective-sample-size factor for a mean.
- The "BH" estimator here, 1+2Σ(1−k/T)r(k), is truncated at the first zero and floored at 1. That is a variance-of-the-mean form, which is not the same as the cross-correlation variance (1+2Σ rx ry) that actually governs the reliability of C(τ).
- The "30 independent samples" interpretation is therefore heuristic.
- The 1/√n and √(2 ln 30) argument is informal.
(4) The calibrated tolerance grows to about 10 % of the lag range (up to ±56 samples). That changes what "stable" means. Scores are then not comparable to published TDS, and the stability judgement becomes coarse, in places wider than the delay itself. This weakens the claim of a "time-scale-consistent TDS".
(5) The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, and its SE treats correlated rotations as independent. Both effects push toward wider tolerances. The authors acknowledge this but do not test block or IAAFT nulls.
(6) Real-data evidence is thin. There are only a handful of single records with "known" delays, and those delays are partly taken from the full-record xcorr peak, which is circular. One of three catchments fails, and oil–gasoline does not discriminate. In the target domain, sleep physiology, there is no improvement.
(7) The synthetic benchmark (AR(1), linear coupling, constant delay) is the setting where TDS is least needed. The time-resolved cases, where TDS should shine, are few and mixed. Sliding-window GCC-PHAT, the natural time-resolved competitor, was not compared.
(8) Presentation: the paper is dense and number-heavy, with many qualifications. It is readable but hard to digest. The main method description is clear (Algorithm 1, Table 1). The disclosure of LLM drafting is noted; it is not an ethical issue per se.

Overall, this is a careful, reproducible, honestly scoped engineering contribution to TDS practice. The conceptual novelty is modest, and the practical advantage over a trivial rescaling is established only for a post hoc variant with its own caveats. I lean toward a borderline reject (5), with high confidence.
