THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of data-driven rules for the three sample-counted parameters of Time Delay Stability (window from a Bartlett-type effective-sample-size factor, lag range = half window capped at half a rhythm period, tolerance calibrated on circularly rotated copies against a 5 % false-stability budget), plus a Bayley–Hammersley window variant (aTDS-BH). The problem is real: TDS parameters (60/±30/±1) are tied to 1-Hz sleep data, and the paper's demonstration that a fixed ±30 lag range yields high scores with wrong (sometimes wrong-sign) delays is a useful, concrete warning for practitioners.

The paper is unusually candid and careful about its own limits. It includes an oracle, a hand-rescaled baseline, an estimated-rescaling baseline, full-record XCorr/GCC-PHAT, sensitivity analyses, held-out validation for aTDS-BH, regime switching, non-monotone coupling, drifting delays, networks with BH-FDR, a 35-subject sleep cohort and five non-physiological records. That breadth is a strength.

However, the honest reporting itself exposes how thin the net contribution is:
- Novelty is low: each ingredient (effective sample size, aliasing limit, surrogate calibration) is standard, and the authors say so. The contribution is engineering a parameter heuristic.
- The default aTDS is essentially matched by a trivial baseline (published setting rescaled by an estimated correlation time); the authors find "no synthetic test in which the default aTDS beats the estimated rescaling with statistical support". The only claimed advantage is one hourly rainfall–runoff record.
- The variant that does win (aTDS-BH) was selected on the slow-down benchmark; the held-out "validation" uses new seeds of the same AR(1) generator family, which is weak evidence of generality. Its gain is explained by the authors as simply longer windows, equivalent to raising the constant 30 to 60 — so it is a tuning knob, not a principled estimator. It also breaches its own 5 % budget (5.2–5.7 %) and fails on regime-limited coupling.
- Full-record estimators dominate on stationary constant-delay data, and on regime-switching coupling the estimated rescaling had far less false stability than aTDS. So the niche where aTDS is uniquely preferable (time-resolved detection) is precisely where its long windows hurt time resolution and leak false stability (8–9 %, above budget) across boundaries.
- On the target domain (sleep network physiology) no adaptive setting improved contrasts; the uncapped window (48 min) could not resolve sleep stages at all; the wake contrast got weaker.
- Real-data "wins" rest on few independent windows (44 windows for hourly rainfall, 19 for temperature–demand), the "known" delays are partly defined via full-record cross-correlation (circular), and aTDS failed on one of three catchments (seasonality) requiring a manual cap.
- The calibrated tolerance grows with time scale (up to ±56 samples, can exceed the delay), so "stability" becomes coarse; aTDS scores are explicitly incomparable with published TDS, which undercuts the reproducibility argument.
- The rotation null and SE that treats correlated rotations as independent are acknowledged as anti-conservative; single recordings reach 20 % chance score.
- All main results use the non-causal stability anchor, which gives 58.1 % vs 43.6 % on the MATLAB benchmark — i.e. not the reference TDS; the causal-anchor rerun is left to future work.
- Minor inconsistency: Limitations say per-link power was measured only in the network test, but Table 5 reports per-link power on the slow-down benchmark. The paper is very dense, with many results relegated to numerical prose and an appendix; the abstract leads with aTDS-BH numbers while the main method is the first-zero version, a slightly selective framing. Single author, AI-drafted, no human expert review reported.

Overall: a careful, honest technical report with a useful practical caution, but limited novelty, a main method that does not beat a simple baseline, a post-hoc winning variant validated only on the same generator family, and no gain in the motivating application. I lean reject, around 4.
