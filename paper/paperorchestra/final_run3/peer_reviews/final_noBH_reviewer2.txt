THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, three data-driven rules for the window (30 x max Bartlett factor), lag range ((L-1)/2 capped at half the rhythm period) and tolerance (widest t with rotation chance score + 2 SE <= 5 %) of Time Delay Stability. The motivation is real. TDS parameters are counted in samples, and the "silent failure" of a fixed +/-30 lag range, which gives high scores with wrong delays, is a useful and clearly shown observation. The paper is also unusually candid. It reports that full-record cross-correlation and GCC-PHAT dominate on constant-delay data. It reports that simply rescaling the published setting by an estimated correlation time is statistically equivalent to aTDS on all synthetic tests and has more per-link power at r = 4 (0.82 vs 0.66). It reports that the oracle beats aTDS at every r >= 2, that the 35-subject sleep cohort shows no gain (and a weaker wake contrast in significant links), that non-monotone coupling is handled worse than by the published setting, and that regime boundaries leak false stability above the budget.

That honesty is commendable, but it also defines the contribution. The main empirical win is over a strawman: the published setting used far outside the regime it was designed for. A trivial baseline that any careful analyst would apply (rescale by the estimated tau_c) does as well. The paper's own statement is: "We found no synthetic test in which the default aTDS beats the estimated rescaling with statistical support". What remains distinctive is the explicit false-stability budget, the rhythm cap and a single rule for mixed-speed signals. These are each standard ingredients, and their benefit is not demonstrated against the rescaling. Evidence that aTDS is superior on real data rests on a single hourly rainfall-runoff record, where the "known" delay is partly defined by full-record cross-correlation (acknowledged as circular). Several real-data results rest on very few windows: 12-19 windows for temperature-demand, 19 for L0123002, and about 44 for the hourly rainfall-runoff record. That undermines the time-resolved rationale for using TDS over full-record estimators at all.

Technical concerns:
(1) The calibrated tolerance grows to about 10 % of the lag range (+/-56 samples at r = 16, +/-136 s in the uncapped cohort). "Stability" then becomes a coarse notion, and scores are no longer comparable with any published TDS value. The method largely trades a strict stability criterion for a chance-calibrated, loose one.
(2) The 5 % budget is on the mean chance score. The SE treats correlated rotations as independent, the rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, and single recordings exceed the budget substantially (95th percentile up to 20 %).
(3) Truncating the Bartlett sum at the first zero crossing makes the window rule fail on seasonal data (L0123002). For long-memory data B depends on record length, so the "intrinsic" window is not well defined.
(4) The constant 30 is shown to be suboptimal for detection (45-60 better), and in sensitivity the chance score then slightly exceeds the budget.
(5) Results use the non-causal stability anchor, which changes the benchmark score from 43.5 % to 58.1 %, and are not repeated with the causal anchor.
(6) The time-resolved advantage is argued but only tested on synthetic regime switching, where the estimated rescaling again did as well with less false stability.

Presentation: the paper is dense and number-heavy, and repetitive in places. The practical guidance repeats bullets, for example the 45-60 independent-samples point and the boundary margin. The extensive Section 4 reads more like a technical report than a focused paper. The disclosure that the manuscript was drafted by an LLM pipeline and not reviewed by a human expert is noted; it is not an ethical issue, but readers will need extra verification. Experimental quality is good overall: bootstrap intervals, TOST equivalence, held-out seeds and FDR-controlled networks.

Net: careful, honest and reproducible, but the methodological novelty is low. The headline benefit is matched by a trivial baseline, and on the target domain (sleep networks) there is no gain. That places the paper below the acceptance bar for a venue that expects a substantive methodological advance. It could be suitable as a software or methods note.
