THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three data-driven rules for choosing the window, lag range and tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS): window = 30 x max Bartlett factor (with a Bayley-Hammersley-type variant, aTDS-BH), lag range = (L-1)/2 capped at half the shortest rhythm period, and tolerance = the widest value whose chance score on circular rotations stays under a 5 % false-stability budget (mean + 2 SE). The evaluation is unusually broad and unusually candid: slow-down benchmark with published/rescaled/oracle baselines, full-record XCorr and GCC-PHAT, an estimated-rescaling baseline, sensitivity analysis, held-out validation of the BH window, networks with BH-FDR, regime-switching/nonlinear/drifting coupling, a 35-subject sleep cohort, and records from hydrology, energy and fuel prices.

My main intuitions:
1. Soundness of individual pieces is good. Each rule has a clear rationale (effective sample size, circular-correlation aliasing limit, surrogate calibration). The Cauchy-Schwarz argument for using max_i B_i is correct for the null variance-inflation factor, though the first-zero truncation makes it an approximation, which the authors admit. The lag-range result (high TDS scores with wrong, edge-of-range delays) is a genuinely useful warning for practitioners.
2. Novelty is limited. The contribution is a parameter-selection heuristic built from textbook ingredients for one niche coupling measure. The authors themselves show that a simple rescaling of the published setting by an estimated correlation time matches default aTDS on essentially every synthetic test ("We found no synthetic test in which the default aTDS beats the estimated rescaling with statistical support"). So the proposed default method's added value over the obvious baseline is not demonstrated; the advantage rests on one hourly rainfall-runoff record.
3. The variant that does win (aTDS-BH) was selected on the slow-down benchmark. The held-out validation uses new seeds of the same generator family, which is a weak form of held-out validation; the other generators (Table 3/10) help, but its gain is "essentially that of a longer window", equivalent to raising the 30 constant to 60, and it exceeds the false-stability budget (5.2-5.7 %) and fails on regime-limited coupling. So the headline abstract numbers (AUC 0.996 vs 0.949) somewhat oversell a choice of a longer window.
4. Where TDS is actually used (1-Hz sleep networks), no adaptive setting helps; the uncapped window is unusable for staging; the wake contrast becomes weaker. On stationary constant-delay data, full-record estimators dominate. The remaining niche — time-resolved delay tracking in systems slower than the 1-Hz setting — is real but the evidence there is synthetic (regime-switching, drift) and the long windows aTDS selects (e.g. 80 days for hourly rainfall, 27 days for half-hourly demand, 48 min for sleep) undercut the time-resolution argument that motivates TDS over full-record estimators in the first place.
5. Tolerance calibration widens tolerance to ~5-10 % of the lag range; "stability" then becomes coarse, scores are no longer comparable to published TDS, and the false-stability budget concerns the mean chance score, not per-link error. The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation and treats correlated rotations as independent — both bias toward wider tolerances (acknowledged).
6. All main results use a non-causal stability anchor that gives 58.1 % vs 43.5 % on the benchmark — a material departure from the original method that is not re-checked.
7. Real-data delay "ground truth" is partly circular (full-record XCorr peak), and there are few records; one catchment fails without a manual cap.
8. Missing comparison: sliding-window GCC-PHAT / sliding-window XCorr with a delay-tracking criterion, which is the natural time-resolved competitor.
9. Presentation: dense, thorough, honest, many numbers in prose; the multiple variants (aTDS, aTDS-BH, rescaled, capped, stage-aware) make the take-home message diffuse. The paper discloses LLM drafting and absence of human expert review — not an ethical issue per se.

Overall: a rigorous, honest engineering study with modest originality and modest demonstrated advantage over a trivial baseline. Major claims are mostly well supported because they are carefully scoped, but the scoping leaves a small contribution. I lean toward reject (5) for a general venue; it would be a reasonable fit for a methods/software journal after tightening.
