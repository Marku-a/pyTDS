THOUGHT:
The paper proposes data-driven rules for the three sample-counted parameters of Time Delay Stability (window, lag range, tolerance): a window holding ~30 effective samples of the slowest signal (first-zero Bartlett factor), a lag range of (L-1)/2 capped at half a rhythm period, and the widest tolerance whose rotation-null chance score (mean + 2 SE) stays below 5 %. A Bayley–Hammersley-type window variant (aTDS-BH) is added. Evaluation is extensive: slow-down AR(1) benchmark against published, hand-rescaled, estimated-rescaled, oracle and full-record estimators; fGn, AR(2) rhythms, Rulkov maps, mixed-speed, changing-dynamics, regime-switching, nonlinear and drifting coupling; networks with rotation p-values and BH-FDR; a 35-subject sleep cohort; and five records from hydrology, energy and economics.

My main intuitions:
- The paper is unusually candid. It reports that default aTDS is statistically indistinguishable from the simplest baseline (published setting rescaled by an estimated correlation time), that full-record cross-correlation/GCC-PHAT dominate on constant-delay data, that the oracle beats aTDS at every r >= 2, that there is no gain on the 1-Hz sleep cohort, that aTDS fails on one catchment and is worse on non-monotone coupling. This honesty is a real strength, but it also strips away most of the claimed contribution.
- What remains is: (a) a fixed lag range can silently report a wrong delay at high scores (useful, though fairly obvious once stated), (b) scaling TDS parameters with the correlation time restores detection on slow signals (expected, and matched by the trivial rescaling), and (c) aTDS-BH detects better mainly because its windows are longer — the paper itself shows that first-zero aTDS with 60 effective samples does almost as well. So the gain of the "validated" variant reduces to "use longer windows", which costs time resolution and breaks regime-limited coupling (Sec. 4.13). The method contribution is thus incremental.
- Methodological concerns: aTDS-BH was selected on the benchmark and then "validated" on new seeds of the same generator family — that is not strong out-of-distribution validation; the other generators are a better test, and there the gains concentrate on long memory where B is record-length dependent. The tolerance calibration uses an SE that treats correlated rotations as independent (acknowledged), and the rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, which is exactly the situation in sleep data. The calibrated tolerances become large (up to ±56 samples, sometimes exceeding the delay), so a "stable" label is coarse, and aTDS scores are no longer comparable with published TDS scores — this undermines one stated motivation (reproducibility/comparability). The 30-effective-sample constant is a convention; sensitivity shows it is the dominant knob, so the rule largely moves the arbitrariness into one constant. The Bartlett bound argument (Eq. 5) is fine in principle but applies to the full sums, while the estimator truncates at the first zero crossing — acknowledged but not quantified beyond the seasonal catchment failure.
- Real-data evidence is thin: the positive cases are two single records (hourly rainfall–runoff, temperature–demand) where the "known" delay is partly defined by full-record cross-correlation (circular reference, acknowledged). One catchment fails. The sleep cohort, the domain the method targets, shows no improvement and some weaker contrasts (wake).
- Some internal inconsistencies to check: Table 7 lists L0123002 aTDS parameters 1059 / 367 / 9, but τmax = (L-1)/2 would be 529 and a seasonal rhythm cap would give ~182; the derivation of 367 is unclear. Table 10's mixed-system row and the "Rescaled by estimated r" equalling published in fGn rows deserve explanation. The Limitations paragraph ends with a garbled sentence about per-link power that conflicts with Table 5. Notation for the sign convention (−τ0) is easy to misread.
- Presentation: dense, number-heavy, with many qualifying follow-ups that make the narrative hard to follow; the abstract and contributions list try to cover too many variants. The paper discloses it was drafted by an LLM pipeline and not reviewed by a human expert. Clarity of individual statements is good, but the overall structure reads like an experiment log.

Overall: careful, reproducible, honest engineering study with modest novelty and a main practical finding that a simple correlation-time rescaling already captures the benefit. Useful as a software/methods note (e.g., for a journal like SoftwareX or a methods section of a physiology paper), but below the bar for a significant methodological contribution. Score 5, leaning reject.
