THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three heuristic rules for choosing the window, lag range and tolerance of Time Delay Stability from the data: a window holding about 30 "independent samples" (Bartlett or Bayley–Hammersley factor), a lag range of half the window capped at half a rhythm period, and a tolerance calibrated on circularly rotated copies against a 5 % false-stability budget. The evaluation is unusually broad and candid. It covers a slow-down benchmark, ablations of each rule, oracle and rescaled baselines, full-record estimators, a sensitivity analysis, held-out validation of the BH variant, per-link power, an FDR network test, regime-switching, nonlinear and drifting coupling, a 35-subject sleep cohort and five records from other fields. The paper reports its negative results openly. Full-record estimators dominate on stationary data. Rescaling by an estimated correlation time roughly matches the default aTDS. aTDS does not help on sleep networks. aTDS-BH exceeds the budget and fails under regime switching.

My central judgement is that the contribution is modest, and the honest negative results themselves show this. The core idea, scaling parameters to the correlation time, is what a careful analyst already does. The paper shows that a one-line rescaling by the 1/e autocorrelation time does as well as the default aTDS on the main benchmark, the mixed-speed system and the drifting delay. The remaining advantage rests mainly on aTDS-BH. That variant was selected on the slow-down benchmark and "validated" on a held-out set from the same AR(1) generator with new seeds, which is weak external validation. aTDS-BH then exceeds the chance budget at r = 8 and 16 and does worse on regime-limited coupling, which is the use case the paper says distinguishes TDS from full-record estimators. So the method is strongest where TDS is least needed (constant, always-on coupling, where GCC/xcorr give AUC 1.0) and weakest where TDS is meant to add value (time-resolved, regime-limited coupling). On the one real application the method was designed for (sleep networks), there is no gain, and the wake contrast is significantly weaker.

Technical concerns:
(1) The "Bartlett factor" 1 + 2 Σ r_i(k)² of a single series, truncated at the first zero crossing, is a heuristic. Bartlett's variance inflation for a cross-correlation involves Σ r_x r_y. The variance of a mean, which is what BH approximates, involves Σ r. The rationale for "30 independent samples" with either estimator is loose, and the paper's own sensitivity analysis shows the constant matters most.
(2) The calibrated tolerance becomes very wide (up to ±56 samples, about 10 % of the lag range, and it can exceed the delay itself). This changes what "stability" means and makes scores incomparable with published TDS. The paper acknowledges this, but it undermines the claimed reproducibility and comparability benefit.
(3) The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, and the SE treats correlated rotations as independent. Both bias towards a wider tolerance. Single recordings exceed the budget substantially (95th percentile up to 20 %).
(4) The synthetic benchmark is constructed so that the published setting must fail (τc and d scale with r). The comparison with the published setting is therefore near-tautological. The meaningful baselines, rescaling and full-record estimators, either match or beat default aTDS.
(5) Main conclusions use the non-causal stability anchor, which changes the benchmark score from 43.5 % to 58.1 %. The paper notes this but does not re-run the study with the causal anchor.
(6) The real-field records are few (n = 5, effectively one success per field), and one catchment fails without a hand-chosen cap.

Presentation: the paper is extremely dense, number-heavy and long for its idea. The many variants (aTDS, aTDS-BH, capped, stage-aware, per-pair joint B, dmax) blur what the recommended method actually is. The practical guidance amounts to "choose the variant and cap depending on your question", which partly reintroduces the hand-tuning the method set out to remove. The writing is otherwise precise and self-critical. The disclosure that a multi-agent LLM pipeline drafted the paper without human expert review is noted; this is not an ethical issue in itself.

Overall: a careful, honest, reproducible engineering study of parameter heuristics for a niche method. The novelty is limited, and the main empirical case for adopting it over simple rescaling or full-record estimators is thin and partly selected on its own benchmark. It is below the bar for acceptance but useful as a methods note or software paper. I score it 5, Reject.
