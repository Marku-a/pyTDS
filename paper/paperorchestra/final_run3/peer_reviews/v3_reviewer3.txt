THOUGHT:
The paper proposes rules for setting the three TDS parameters (window, lag range, tolerance) from the data. The window targets about 30 effective samples via a first-zero-truncated Bartlett factor. The lag range is (L-1)/2, capped at half a rhythm period. The tolerance is the widest one whose rotation-surrogate chance score (mean + 2 SE) stays within a 5% budget. The question is practical and real: TDS parameters are counted in samples, so their meaning changes with time scale and sampling rate, and that hurts reproducibility. The paper is unusually candid and thorough. It reports many negative results, runs sensitivity analyses, compares against full-record estimators, validates the BH variant on held-out data and reports equivalence tests. That honesty is a strength, but it also exposes how small the contribution is.

Main points for my assessment:
1. Novelty is modest. Each ingredient is textbook (effective sample size, the aliasing limit of the circular correlation, rotation surrogates). By the paper's own account, the default aTDS is statistically indistinguishable from the simplest baseline: the published setting rescaled by an estimated correlation time (r_hat = tau_c/2). "We find no test in which it is better with statistical support." The only edge is one hourly rainfall-runoff record. So the headline method does not beat a trivial heuristic.
2. The better variant, aTDS-BH, was chosen on the benchmark. Its gain is "essentially that of a longer window" and is reproduced by simply raising the constant from 30 to 60. The window rule therefore reduces to "use longer windows when time resolution doesn't matter", which the oracle already shows. aTDS-BH also exceeds the false-stability budget and fails in regime-switching settings. The BH factor is the variance inflation of a sample mean, not of a cross-correlation, so its theoretical motivation is weak; the authors say so themselves.
3. The tolerance rule raises interpretability problems. The tolerance becomes as wide as ±56 samples, up to about 10% of the lag range, and can exceed the delay itself. Scores are then not comparable with published TDS values, nor across settings. TDS's main appeal is a stable delay as a physical quantity. A ±56 stability band weakens the "stable delay" meaning. Also, the tolerance-only ablation gives no significant AUC gain.
4. Null-model issues. Rotation surrogates destroy shared slow co-modulation, and the SE assumes independent rotations. Both biases push towards a wider tolerance. The authors acknowledge this but do not test block or IAAFT nulls or a clustered SE. Single recordings exceed the budget (95th percentile up to 20%), and regime boundaries leak false stability (8-9%).
5. Real-data evidence is thin. In the 35-subject sleep cohort (the target domain), no gain appears, and the wake contrast is weaker. The other-field results rest on single records with very few windows (12/12, 19/19, 35/44 stable windows). One catchment fails because of seasonality, and oil-gasoline does not discriminate. The "known delays" partly come from the full-record cross-correlation, which is circular.
6. Synthetic benchmarks are mostly linear AR(1) with always-on coupling, where full-record estimators win (AUC 1.0). The time-resolved advantage that should justify TDS is shown in only one synthetic regime-switching test, and there the estimated rescaling does better on false stability.
7. Methodological details: the Cauchy–Schwarz bound argument is correct for the full sums, but the first-zero truncation breaks the bound. The non-causal vs causal anchor gives 58.1% vs 43.5% on the benchmark pair, yet all main results use the non-causal anchor. That choice is a sizeable, unexplored confound.
8. Clarity: the writing is dense and number-heavy. Results are scattered across sections and appendix tables, with forward references (Table 10 and Table 11 are cited before Table 3). The many variants make the take-home message diffuse. It reads as a technical report more than a focused paper. The disclosure that a pipeline drafted the paper and no human expert has reviewed it is noted; this is not an ethics problem per se.

Overall: careful, honest and reproducible engineering work, with useful practical guidance (for example, the lag-range silent-failure finding is valuable). But the proposed default does not beat a trivial baseline, the better variant reduces to a longer window, and the target application shows no gain. The contribution is marginal-to-modest. Leaning reject at a selective venue; a better fit would be a methods or software note.
