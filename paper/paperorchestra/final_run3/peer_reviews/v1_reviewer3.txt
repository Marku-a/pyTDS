THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of heuristic rules that set the three sample-counted parameters of Time Delay Stability (window, lag range, tolerance) from the data: window = 30 x (max Bartlett variance-inflation factor), lag range = (L-1)/2 capped at half a rhythm period, and tolerance = widest value whose rotation-null chance score (mean + 2 SE) stays below a 5 % false-stability budget. A Bayley-Hammersley variant (aTDS-BH) is identified post hoc and checked on a held-out seed set. The evaluation is very extensive (slow-down AR(1) benchmark, fGn, AR(2) rhythms, Rulkov maps, mixed speed, regime switching, nonlinear and drifting coupling, networks with BH-FDR, a 35-subject sleep cohort, and hydrology/energy/fuel records). The writing is unusually candid about limitations.

My main reservations:
1. Novelty and significance are modest. The core idea, scaling a windowed estimator to the effective sample size and calibrating a threshold on circular-shift surrogates, is standard practice. The paper's own strongest baseline, the published setting rescaled by an estimated correlation time, matches the original aTDS on the slow-down benchmark (undecided or equivalent at most r) and on several other tests, and does better in the regime-switching case (2.0 % vs 8.1 % false stability). Most of the gain over the published setting is therefore explained by "rescale by the time scale", which a careful analyst would already do.
2. The variant that carries the headline claims (aTDS-BH) was chosen on the benchmark. The held-out set uses new seeds of the same generator, so it is not out-of-distribution validation. aTDS-BH also exceeds the false-stability budget at r = 8 and 16 and does worse when coupling is regime-limited, which is the use case that justifies TDS over full-record estimators.
3. Positioning is tense. Full-record cross-correlation and GCC-PHAT dominate on stationary data (AUC 1.0 at every r). TDS's justification is therefore time resolution. But aTDS deliberately lengthens windows (48 min in the sleep cohort; uncapped, it cannot resolve stages). On the original target domain, staged sleep networks, it brings no gain, and the wake contrast is weaker. The time-resolved advantage rests mainly on synthetic regime-switching and drift tests. Sliding-window GCC-PHAT, the natural time-resolved competitor, was not compared.
4. Widening the tolerance (up to about 10 % of the lag range, ±56 samples) changes what "stable" means. Scores become incomparable with the TDS literature, and stability can be coarser than the delay itself. This weakens the claim to be "TDS" with automatic parameters rather than a different statistic. A tolerance tied to the variance of the delay estimate is listed only as future work.
5. Methodological details:
   - The main results use the non-causal stability anchor, which gives 58.1 % vs the reference 43.6 % on the benchmark pair. The causal anchor was not re-run.
   - The 2-SE rule treats correlated rotations as independent.
   - The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation.
   - The 30-sample constant is conservative; 45-60 detects better.
   - Window selection by the slowest signal penalises fast pairs.
6. The real-data evidence is thin. There are three non-physiology records, with one failure (a seasonal catchment) and one non-discriminating record. The delays there are also recoverable by full-record cross-correlation, which is how the "known" delays were defined.

Strengths include the rigorous statistics (paired bootstrap, TOST equivalence, Wilson intervals), the demonstration of the silent wrong-delay failure of a fixed lag range, reproducibility (code, seeds), and a very honest limitations section. Clarity is good, though the paper is dense and reads like an exhaustive technical report with many caveats. It also states it was drafted by an LLM pipeline and has not been reviewed by a human expert, which is disclosed and not an ethical problem per se.

Overall: a careful, honest engineering study with a useful practical recipe. The contribution is incremental, the advantage over a simple estimated rescaling is limited to the post-hoc BH variant, and the method brings no gain in its home domain. I lean towards rejection at a selective venue (score 5); it would suit a methods/software journal.
