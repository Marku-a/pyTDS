THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, three data-driven rules for the window length, lag range and tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS). The window holds 30 Bartlett-effective samples of the slowest signal. The lag range is (L-1)/2, capped at half a rhythm period. The tolerance is the widest one whose rotation-null chance score (mean + 2 SE) stays within a 5 % budget. The evaluation is unusually broad and unusually candid. It covers a slow-down benchmark with published, hand-rescaled, estimated-rescaling and oracle baselines, a held-out replication, six signal families, a sensitivity analysis of the constants, network FDR tests, regime-switching/nonlinear/drift scenarios, a 35-subject sleep cohort and five non-physiological records.

My main intuition: the honest reporting removes most of the claimed contribution. The key comparison is against "published setting rescaled by an estimated correlation time". That is a one-line heuristic any careful analyst would apply. The paper itself finds no synthetic test where aTDS beats it with statistical support (Sec. 4.10). The rescaling is numerically better at r=4 in both the main and held-out sets, and it has more per-link power at r=4 (0.82 vs 0.66). The abstract and conclusion still call the two "equivalent", but no TOST is reported for the held-out set or for Table 4. Section 4.10 contradicts equivalence at r=4 for per-link power.

The three claimed "additions" are not shown to help:
- The rhythm cap made aTDS report the wrong delay for the rhythm with d=30, where the rescaling got it right.
- The single mixed-speed rule matched the rescaling.
- The false-alarm budget is real, but it is a budget on the mean. The paper shows single recordings at a 95th percentile up to 20 %, and false stability of 8-9 % near regime boundaries. A per-link test is needed anyway, and that test could be applied to any setting.

The only real-data advantage over the rescaling is one hourly rainfall-runoff record (8 h vs 14 h). The reference delay there is itself partly defined by the full-record cross-correlation. In the target domain (1 Hz sleep networks) no adaptive setting improved the contrasts. One catchment failed outright without a manual cap.

Technical concerns:
(1) The calibrated tolerance grows to about 10 % of the lag range (±56 samples at r=16), which can exceed the delay itself. "Stable" then means something much coarser than in TDS. The scores become incomparable to the literature, and the method partly redefines the statistic rather than tuning it. The oracle picks tolerance 0 with long windows, which suggests that widening the tolerance is not the natural route to good detection.
(2) The Cauchy-Schwarz bound justifying max_i B_i holds for the full sums. With per-signal truncation at different first-zero crossings, B_i no longer strictly bounds the pair factor. Ignoring negative and seasonal lobes caused the L0123002 failure. For long memory, B depends on record length, so the window is not a system property.
(3) The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, and its SE treats correlated rotations as independent. Both push the tolerance wider, as the authors acknowledge, yet the real-data analyses rely on it.
(4) The "silent failure" of a fixed lag range is straightforward: if the delay lies outside the search, the argmax pins at the edge. It is a useful warning, not a finding.
(5) The constant 30 is admitted to be a convention. The sensitivity table shows it is the most consequential constant, and 45-60 is better for detection, so the default is not well chosen for the stated use.
(6) Full-record estimators dominate on the main benchmark. Sliding-window GCC-PHAT, the natural time-resolved competitor, was not compared.

Clarity: the writing is precise but extremely dense. The text is saturated with numbers, and the practical guidance repeats itself (the window-length advice appears twice). Its many qualifications make the paper read like an extended technical report. The AI-drafting disclosure is fine.

Overall: rigorous and honest engineering work, with a modest and largely negative incremental contribution relative to a trivial baseline. Novelty is low, since each ingredient is standard and the combination performs like simple rescaling. Significance is limited to TDS users with non-1 Hz data. I lean reject, around 4.
