THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, three data-driven rules that set the window (30 Bartlett-effective samples of the slowest signal), the lag range (half the window, capped at half a rhythm period) and the tolerance (widest value keeping the rotation-surrogate chance score, mean + 2 SE, at or below 5 %) for Time Delay Stability. The problem is real. TDS parameters are counted in samples and the published 60/±30/±1 values do not carry over to other sampling rates. The finding that a fixed ±30 lag range gives high TDS scores at wrong, edge-of-range delays is a useful warning for practitioners.

The paper is unusually candid, and its own evidence limits the contribution:
(1) On the main synthetic benchmark, full-record cross-correlation and GCC-PHAT reach AUC 1.0 at every r. The published setting rescaled by a simple estimated time scale (1/e autocorrelation lag) matches the rescaling by the true r, and aTDS is "undecided" against that rescaling at r = 4 and 16. So the core adaptive idea is largely achieved by a one-line heuristic.
(2) On the 35-subject sleep cohort, the motivating application domain, aTDS gives no significant gain and weakens the wake contrast. The uncapped window (48 min) cannot resolve stages at all.
(3) The claimed niche is time-resolved coupling (regime switching, drift). It is shown only on synthetic data, and only against the unrescaled published setting, not against the estimated-rescaled baseline that matched aTDS elsewhere. That baseline is the obvious competitor, so this comparison is the main missing experiment. False stability in the uncoupled regime (8–9 %) also exceeds the method's own 5 % budget.
(4) The real-data evidence outside physiology is thin: single records per field, one of three catchments failed, and oil–gasoline does not discriminate. In the hourly rainfall case, aTDS uses an 80-day window and a 1.8-day tolerance to recover an 8-h delay. A "stable" label is then very coarse, and the stable window carries little time resolution.

Technically the rules are reasonable but heuristic. The Bartlett sum is truncated at the first zero crossing. The max-B rule penalises fast pairs. The SE in the tolerance rule ignores correlation between rotations. The rotation null removes shared slow co-modulation, which biases the tolerance wider. The authors acknowledge all of these. The main synthetic model (AR(1) with exponential ACF, y a delayed noisy copy) is the favourable case for a Bartlett-based window. The constant 30 is admittedly a convention, and 45–60 detects better. The statistics are well done: bootstrap AUC intervals, TOST equivalence, Wilson intervals, BH-FDR, train/test separation, and a sensitivity analysis.

Presentation is clear but dense, with many numbers. Some sentences are hard to parse, and the limitations paragraph is run-on.

Overall: a careful, honest engineering contribution of modest novelty and limited demonstrated advantage. It suits a software or methods-note venue better than a main-track paper. I lean reject, with Overall 4.
