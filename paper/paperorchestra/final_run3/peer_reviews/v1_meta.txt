THOUGHT:
All three reviewers agree closely: each gives Overall 5, Confidence 4 and Reject, with Originality 2, Quality 3, Significance 2 and Contribution 2. The only difference is Clarity/Presentation, where Reviewer 3 gives 3 and Reviewers 1 and 2 give 2. Their arguments overlap a great deal, and I share their view.

What is good: the paper takes on a real reproducibility problem. TDS parameters are counted in samples and do not carry over across time scales or sampling rates. The paper proposes three simple, label-free rules for the window, the lag range and the tolerance. The evaluation is unusually broad and fair: per-rule ablations, an oracle, true and estimated rescaling, full-record xcorr and GCC-PHAT, paired bootstrap AUCs, TOST, Wilson intervals and sensitivity analyses. Negative results are reported openly. All reviewers value the finding that a fixed ±30 lag range gives high TDS scores with wrong, even sign-flipped, delays. Seeds and code are released.

Why it falls short of acceptance:
(1) Marginal gain over a trivial baseline. Rescaling the published setting by an estimated 1/e correlation time matches default aTDS on the main benchmark and on several other tests. It beats aTDS under regime switching (2.0% vs 8.1% false stability) and on the long-delay rhythm. The wins over the published setting largely follow from how the slow-down benchmark is built.
(2) The headline advantage rests on aTDS-BH. That variant was selected on the AR(1) benchmark and "held out" only with new seeds from the same generator. It exceeds the 5% budget at r=8 and r=16 and degrades under regime-limited coupling. Its benefit may simply come from longer windows, which the oracle already prefers.
(3) Positioning tension. TDS is justified over full-record estimators by its time resolution. Yet aTDS lengthens windows, loses stage resolution without a manual cap, leaks false stability near regime boundaries, and is not compared with sliding-window xcorr or GCC-PHAT. On the 35-subject sleep cohort, the motivating application, it gives no gain, and the wake contrast is weaker.
(4) The statistical footing is heuristic. The first-zero-truncated 1+2Σr² factor is not the Bartlett cross-correlation factor Σ r_x r_y, so the target of "30 independent samples" is a convention. Calibrated tolerances reach about ±56 samples, which changes what "stable" means and breaks comparability with published TDS values. The rotation null ignores shared co-modulation and treats correlated rotations as independent.
(5) Main results use a non-causal stability anchor (58.1% vs 43.6% on the benchmark pair). Real-data delay recovery is thin and partly circular. Presentation is dense, and the many variants blur which default the paper recommends.

Taking the reviewers together with my own reading, this is a careful, honest engineering study with limited novelty and limited practical gain over simple baselines. My decision is Reject at Overall 5, encouraging resubmission with: an independent generator family to validate aTDS-BH, sliding-window competitors, causal-anchor results, a principled ESS/tolerance derivation, and a clearer single recommendation.
