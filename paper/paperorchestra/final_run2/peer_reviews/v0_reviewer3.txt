THOUGHT:
The paper proposes aTDS, a set of three heuristic rules that set the window length, lag range and tolerance of Time Delay Stability (TDS) from the data: a window holding about 30 Bartlett-effective samples of the slowest signal, a lag range of half the window (capped at half a detected period), and a tolerance chosen as the widest value whose rotation-null chance score (mean + 2 SE) stays below a 5 % false-stability budget. The problem is real. TDS parameters are counted in samples and copied from 1 Hz sleep data, and the demonstration that a fixed ±30 lag range gives high TDS scores with wrong (sometimes sign-flipped) delays is a useful, concrete warning to the field.

My overall impression is that the paper is unusually honest and careful about its claims. It has equivalence tests, bootstrap intervals, an oracle upper bound, a hand-rescaled baseline, explicit train/test separation, and a limitations section that admits mixed real-data results. The methodological novelty is modest, though. Each rule is a standard idea (Bartlett effective sample size, Nyquist-like lag limit for circular correlation, surrogate calibration), and the combination is a sensible engineering recipe rather than a conceptual advance. The empirical case against the strongest fair baseline (a setting rescaled by the true slow-down factor) is weak: equivalent at r = 1, 2, better only at r = 8, undecided at r = 4 and 16, and the oracle is better at every r >= 2. Most of the dramatic gains over the published setting come from the trivial observation that a 60-sample window is too short for slow signals. Almost any time-scale-aware rule would give them.

Specific technical concerns:
- Window rule. B_i is a truncated sum of r^2 up to the first zero crossing. This is a crude estimator: it is sensitive to record length for long-memory signals (acknowledged: 13 to 84) and to rhythmic signals, whose first zero crossing comes early. The "30" constant is justified heuristically, and the on/off test suggests 45-60 is often better.
- Tolerance rule. The calibrated tolerance grows with the time scale (±56 at r = 16, ±136 s in the cohort) and can exceed the delay itself. This weakens what "time delay stability" means. A tolerance of a quarter of the lag range makes the 4-of-5 rule close to a "peak lies in the same broad region" test. The paper partly concedes this. The rotation null ignores shared slow co-modulation, and its SE treats correlated rotations as independent. Both effects favour wider tolerances. The 2-SE margin, the 5 % budget, 20 rotations and the 61 floor were not varied.
- Comparability. aTDS scores are not comparable with published TDS scores, or across datasets with different chosen tolerances. This undermines one of the stated motivations (reproducibility and comparability).
- Real sleep data, the main target domain. The uncapped method fails (a 48-min window). The capped variant uses a manual cap, i.e. a hand-chosen parameter, and does not significantly improve contrasts. The removal of the REM < DS inversion is presented as notable, but with no ground truth it cannot be called an improvement. The wake contrast gets significantly worse for significant links (gain −1.17).
- Other-field records. These are positive, but the windows are enormous (80 days to find an 8-h delay). This is close to full-record cross-correlation, which would itself find the delay. They show the published 1 Hz-tuned setting is mismatched, not that aTDS is a good TDS parameterization. The oil–gasoline record is non-discriminating.
- Synthetic design. The test bed is mostly linear AR(1) with constant delay and permanently-on coupling, which suits the max-correlation estimator. The network test is small (10 networks per design), and the FDP of 8.3 % is inconclusive.
- Presentation. Results are densely written and number-heavy. The Bartlett 1946 reference is wrongly listed as "In arXiv". The manuscript states it is LLM-drafted and not yet reviewed by a human expert. That is not an ethics problem per se, since it is disclosed, but it is noted.

Overall: a solid, honest, reproducible methods note with limited novelty and mixed evidence in the main application domain. It would fit a specialised methods/software venue after revision. At a selective venue I lean to reject. Score 5.
