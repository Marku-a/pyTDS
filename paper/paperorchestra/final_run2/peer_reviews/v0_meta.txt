THOUGHT:
All three reviews are identical in content and scores: Originality 2, Quality 3, Clarity 3, Significance 2, Soundness 3, Presentation 3, Contribution 2, Overall 6, Confidence 4, and Accept from each. Because they are identical, they give one assessment rather than three independent ones. I therefore weight my own reading of the arguments more heavily than the apparent unanimity.

Case for acceptance: TDS parameters are counted in samples and were tuned for 1 Hz sleep data, yet they are reused across systems. This is a real reproducibility problem, and aTDS addresses it with three rules that each have a stated statistical basis and none of which uses labels. The evaluation is strong. Each rule is tested on its own, and the baselines are fair: the published setting, a setting rescaled with the true slow-down factor, and a labelled oracle. The statistics are appropriate (paired bootstrap, TOST, Wilson intervals, a train/test split for calibration). The lag-range result, where fixed TDS gives high scores with a wrong edge delay and sometimes the wrong sign, is a useful practical finding on its own. The paper reports negative results honestly and limits its claims to match.

Case against: the conceptual novelty is modest, since the work combines standard heuristics. The calibrated tolerance grows with the time scale and can exceed the delay itself, which changes what "stable" means, so aTDS scores cannot be compared with published TDS scores. The main physiological use case needed an ad hoc 300 s cap. In that cohort aTDS gives no significant gain on the key contrast and loses the wake contrast. The removal of the REM < DS inversion is not validated against ground truth. The rotation null is known to be anti-conservative when signals share slow co-modulation. The paper also does not compare against generic delay estimators such as GCC/PHAT, and it does not test how sensitive the results are to its fixed constants.

My judgement: this is a solid, careful methods and reproducibility paper. It has limited novelty and mixed real-world gains. The rigour, the documented silent-failure mode and the transparency make it a useful contribution, but it is borderline rather than strong. I keep Overall at 6 and the Accept decision. I lower Confidence slightly to 3, because the three reviews are identical and so give little independent corroboration. The camera-ready version should address the meaning of the tolerance, the bias of the null, the GCC baseline and the sensitivity to the fixed constants.
